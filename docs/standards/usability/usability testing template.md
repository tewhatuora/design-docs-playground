# Usability testing template

The best way to know if something works is to watch someone use it. Even a few sessions with real users asking them to think out loud will show you problems you wouldn't spot on your own.

**This template is for testing with Health NZ staff and health professionals only** and includes all the MUSTs and the SHOULDs from the usability testing standards. Testing with consumers or the public needs a more robust process, including information sheets, signed consent and koha.

## How to use this template

Copy this template into a Word document and fill it in as you go. Replace anything in [square brackets] with your own details. It's a starting point, so cut it down or adapt it to suit your needs.

1. [Plan](#1-plan): decide what to test and set up the test. Then copy your scenarios into the test script.
2. [Run the test (test script)](#2-run-the-test-test-script): follow the test script.
3. [Report results and act on feedback](#3-report-results-and-act-on-feedback): share findings and agree fixes with your team.

[Prototype link or test environment link.]

### Test data

[Write down everything participants need for each scenario, so it's ready before the session. For example, test logins and passwords, and fictional names, numbers or records. Never use real patient or staff information.]

### Before the session

- Do a practice run with a colleague first to check the prototype works and the scenarios make sense.
- Have your prototype and test data ready.
- Ask a colleague to take notes in the notes table, including whether each task was a pass or fail. This lets you focus on the participant, and you can both add to the notes afterwards. If you're on your own, record the session and take notes afterwards.
- Agree as a team what you'll ask consent for: what you'll record (for example video and/or their screen), who will see it, where you'll store it, how long you'll keep it, and whether their name goes in an engagement catalogue. Usually only the product team sees recordings. Only keep or share them more widely if the participant explicitly consents.

## 2. Run the test (test script)

Follow this test script. Anything after **Say:** is what you read out. Everything else is a note for you.

**Tips for the facilitator:**

- Your main job is to build rapport and help the participant feel comfortable.
- This script is a guide, not a checklist. Read the room and don't force things if it makes the participant feel uncomfortable.
- Any feedback is better than no feedback. It's fine if you don't get through everything.
- Decide how long the session will be up front, and stick to it. 45 minutes is a good starting point.

### Step 1: Welcome

> **Say:**
>
> - Hi [name], how are you doing today? Thanks so much for helping us out with this.
> - I'm [your name], and this is [name], who'll be taking notes. We are [describe your role and what you do, briefly].
> - I'll just give you a bit of an overview of what we're doing today before we get you to introduce yourself.
> - Have you taken part in a usability test before? Even though it's called a test, we're testing [product], not you. There are no right or wrong answers. It's just an informal session to get your feedback.
> - I'm going to show you [product] and ask you to complete some tasks, and I'll ask you a few questions along the way.
> - As you go, please think out loud as much as you can. Say what you're looking at, what you're trying to do and what you're thinking.
> - If you'd like to stop or take a break at any point, just let me know.

### Step 2: Consent

Be clear about exactly what you're asking consent for, using what you agreed in your plan.

> **Say:**
>
> - Do you mind if I record this session? We'll record [audio, video and your screen]. Only [the product team] will see it, and we'll keep it for [how long].
> - Are you happy for your name to be added to our engagement catalogue, so we can keep track of who we've spoken to?
> - Any questions before we record?

If they agree, start recording now. Record their consent in writing or on the recording.

The engagement catalogue is your programme's record of who you've spoken to. If your programme doesn't keep one, leave that question out or start one.

### Step 3: Warm-up

> **Say:**  
> Great, now that we're recording, we'd love to hear a bit about you. Could you introduce yourself, including your role and how long you've been working there?

Then ask a few of these if you need more context:

> **Say:**
>
> - Can you describe a typical day/week for you?
> - How would you describe your experience with [product]? New to it, some experience, or experienced?
> - How often do you use [product]? First time or rarely, sometimes, or most days?

### Step 4: Tasks

> **Say:**  
> Thanks, that's really helpful. Now I'm going to read out a few scenarios, one at a time. Please try each one on [product] as you normally would and try to think out loud as you go.

If you're online, ask them to share their screen now.

> **Say:**  
> Scenario 1: [copy from your plan, for example: "You'd like to take Friday 20 December off as annual leave. Request the day off."]

If they go quiet or ask you for help, you then ask a few of these:

> **Say:**
>
> - What are you thinking now?
> - What are you looking for?
> - What would you do if I wasn't here?
> - What did you expect to happen?

Record whether they:

- completed it (pass)
- completed it with help (pass with help)
- didn't complete it (fail)

Optional, after a task:

> **Say:**  
> Was that easy, OK or hard? Why?

Repeat for each scenario.

### Step 5: Wrap-up

> **Say:**
>
> - Thanks so much, that's all the scenarios done. How was that for you?
> - I've just got a few quick questions to finish off.

Optional questions:

> **Say:**
>
> - Overall, how was [product] to use? Easy, OK or hard?
> - Why?

Optional, if the product replaces something:

> **Say:**
>
> - Compared with what you use now, is it better, the same or worse?
> - Why?

Before you finish, give them a chance to raise anything you missed, then thank them:

> **Say:**  
> Is there anything about [product] we haven't talked about that you'd like to mention?

> **Say:**  
> Thanks again for your time today and sharing your feedback. It's been great learning about you and what you do. Have a great rest of your day!
