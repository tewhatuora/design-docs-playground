# Accessibility

A new Digital Accessibility Standard is expected in early 2027. It will cover all digital products and services, not just websites. This page explains what's changing and where to find out more.

## About accessibility

The New Zealand Government's definition of web accessibility is:

*"Web accessibility is about making web content that disabled people can use." ([Web Accessibility Guide](https://govtnz.github.io/web-a11y-guidance/ka/fundamental-concepts-in-web-a11y/what-is-web-a11y))*

It considers things like:

- the different types of devices that disabled people use
- the range of permanent, temporary and situational impairments and disabilities that people may experience
- people's changing abilities due to ageing.

Fundamentally, web accessibility is about designing web content so that it doesn't present any barriers to disabled people accessing and using it.

For a quick overview, watch the [4-minute introduction to web accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/videos/standards-and-benefits/) from the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C).

## About accessibility standards

The current standard is the Web Accessibility Standard 1.2, in place since March 2025. It's based on the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, the international guidelines for making web content accessible, and only covers websites.

The [Government Digital Delivery Agency (GDDA)](https://www.publicservice.govt.nz/about-the-commission/government-digital-delivery-agency) is replacing it with a new Digital Accessibility Standard, created *"to make sure that accessibility is considered for all digital technology, not just websites."* The draft went out for consultation from 13 July to 7 August 2026, and the final version is expected in early 2027.

The draft says *"Each ICT [information and communication technology] Product and Service must meet"* EN 301 549 version 4.1.1, a European accessibility standard. That includes web pages, documents, non-web software (including mobile apps) and hardware. It will apply to new products from the start date, with 1 to 5 years for existing products to catch up.

## What this means for Health NZ

The new standard goes beyond websites. As it's finalised, we'll be looking at what it means for all of Health NZ's digital products and services, including workforce apps, documents and other software.

Accessibility needs to be considered at every stage of a project, starting from the beginning. It's much harder to make a service accessible once foundational decisions have been made.
